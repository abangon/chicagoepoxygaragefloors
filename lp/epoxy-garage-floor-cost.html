DIY kit vs. hiring a pro

A store kit runs a few hundred dollars and a weekend of work. The catch is prep: most kits rely on acid etching, which does not open the concrete as well as a diamond grinder. The result is a thinner coat that is more likely to lift under hot tires, especially with Chicago road salt.

If the garage is a workshop you plan to redo anyway, a kit can be fine. If you want the floor to last, grinding and a full multi-coat system are what you pay a pro for.

FAQ

Price questions

How much does epoxy flooring cost for a 2-car garage?
About $3,600–$5,500 for a full-flake epoxy floor on a 2-car garage (400–500 sq ft) in decent shape. Polyaspartic is $4,000–$7,500. Crack repair, old coating removal or moisture treatment add to that.
How much for a 3-car garage?
About $5,400–$8,250 for full-flake epoxy on 600–750 sq ft.
Is it cheaper to do it myself?
Store kits cost a few hundred dollars, but most are thin water-based epoxy over acid-etched concrete. They often lift under hot tires within a couple of years. A professional floor costs more because of diamond grinding and a thick, multi-coat system.
Does an epoxy garage floor add value to a home?
It makes the garage look finished and is easy to keep clean, which buyers notice. We have not seen solid data on how much it raises a sale price, so we do not promise a number.
Why can't you give me an exact price over the phone?
Two garages of the same size can differ by hundreds of dollars because of cracks, old paint, oil or moisture. A photo helps us narrow it down; the on-site visit lets us write a firm price.
